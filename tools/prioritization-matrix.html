How to use an effort/impact matrix

When more work is asking for attention than you can deliver, the effort/impact matrix gives you a defensible order in minutes. You judge each initiative on two things: the effort to deliver it and the impact if you do. Where it lands tells you what to do with it, and the four quadrants map cleanly to four moves.

Do the quick wins first. They are low effort and high impact, so they build momentum and credibility cheaply, which buys you room for the bigger bets. Plan the major projects deliberately, because high impact at high effort is worth it but only if you fund it properly. Use fill-ins to absorb spare capacity. And be honest about the thankless tasks, high effort for low impact, which you defer or drop unless something genuinely forces them.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with the major project. The big bet feels important, but shipping a few quick wins first earns the trust and the runway to do it right.
  • Letting everything become high impact. If it is all high impact, nothing is. Be willing to call work low impact even when someone loves it.
  • Underestimating effort on things you want to do. Wishful effort estimates quietly turn major projects into quick wins on paper, then reality corrects you mid-quarter.
  • Treating the grid as the final ranking. It aligns a group on rough order fast. For fine trade-offs across many factors, use a weighted decision matrix.
